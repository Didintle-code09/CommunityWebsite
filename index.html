Chapter 01

The Land

Where we are.

The land

Between Seshego and Moletjie

Mmotong wa Perekisi sits north-west of Polokwane, in the Capricorn District of Limpopo. Maps and records also call it Perskebult, and some addresses write Perskebuilt.

Sources: Mmotong FM on Zeno.FM, which describes the village as lying between Seshego and Moletjie; Census 2011, Main Place 974041; Graceland Combined School.

Thirteen square kilometres

The 2011 census drew Perskebult as 13.04 km² of land. Its centre lies about 14 km north-west of central Polokwane, as the crow flies.

Source: Census 2011, Main Place 974041. Distance measured from the census boundary to Polokwane’s published coordinates.

Two sub-places, one community

Inside the boundary, the census counts two sub-places: Ga-Mokgokong, home to 10 644 people, and Perskebult, home to 6 361. School and business addresses add more local names, like Ga-Mabotja and Makgofe.

Sources: Census 2011; Mphareng Primary and Mohlapetse Secondary school listings.

The road through

New Era Drive, part of the R567, passes through Perskebult. The same road carries on to villages like Mabokelele, about 25 km north-west of Polokwane.

Sources: map listing on New Era Dr (R567), Perskebult; Wikipedia, Mabokelele.

Land and water

Maps mark a Perskebult Dam. The hills, fields and footpaths that shaped daily life here, and how people first came to settle this land

Source: map listing for Perskebult Dam.

What’s in a name

Perskebult. Perskeperske means peach in Afrikaansbultbult means a rise or a ridge

Perekisi, as in Mmotong wa Perekisi, is the Sesotho word for peach.

Mmotong

The name represents a hilly village populated with peaches(Perekisi).

Also written as Perskebult, Perskebuilt, Mmotong wa Perekisi and Mmotong wa Perskebult.

Sources: Afrikaans dictionary meanings; Sesotho–English dictionary entry for perekisi; spellings as used in census, school and business records.

Before

Where we came from.

  1. 1962A territorial authority is set up for Lebowa

A clue in the school records

Official school listings name the former education department of two local schools, Mphareng Primary and Mohlapetse Secondary, as Lebowa: the apartheid-era homeland created for Northern Sotho speakers. Nearby Seshego was Lebowa’s capital from 1972 to 1974. Lebowa was abolished in 1994.

Sources: Mphareng Primary listing; Mohlapetse Secondary listing; Wikipedia, Seshego; South African History Online.

A young community

In 2011, more than half of Perskebult’s residents were under 25. They are the ones who will carry this story forward.

Out of every hundred residents, about fifty-five were under 25.

Source: Census 2011 age breakdown for Perskebult (9 278 of 17 006 residents).

Have a story?

This website exists to keep the history of Mmotong / Perskebuilt in the hands of the people who lived it. Soon, you’ll be able to add to it.

What you’ll be able to share

  • Stories
  • Memories
  • Family histories
  • Names and dates
  • Place names
  • Sayings and songs, written down
  • Historical information
  • Corrections

Contributions open soon

Online sharing isn’t live yet. Until then, write down names, dates and places, and ask elders to tell their stories while they can.

How it will work

  1. Share a story, a memory or a correction, in English or Sepedi.
  2. Check together. Names, dates and places are confirmed with you before anything is published.
  3. Credit. With your permission, it joins the archive under your name.
